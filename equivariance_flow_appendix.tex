\section{Experimental design: walking the attribution directions}
\label{app:equivariance-flow}

Full detail for the experiment summarised in
\S\ref{sec:equivariance-flow}, implemented in
\texttt{aep\_core\_annulus\_equivariance\_flow.py}. The \textsc{resolved} and
\textsc{fixed} modes write to
\texttt{outputs/aep\_core\_annulus\_equivariance\_flow[\_fixed]} respectively.

\subsection{Model and data}

The annulus classifier of \texttt{aep\_core\_annulus\_mlp.py}: an MLP
$f_\theta:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ (hidden width $96$, depth $4$, GELU,
$19{,}009$ parameters), trained by binary cross-entropy (Adam, learning rate
$3\times10^{-3}$, $200$ epochs, no augmentation or equivariance penalty) to
separate a filled unit disc ($y=+1$, $100$ points, $r\le1$) from an outer
annular band ($y=-1$, $100$ points, $r\in[2.3,3.0]$). The rule depends only
on $r$ and is therefore exactly $\mathrm{SO}(2)$-invariant. Two datasets vary
the angular extent of the outer band: a $90^\circ$ wedge (final loss
$6.4\times10^{-7}$) and the full $360^\circ$ annulus (final loss
$8.9\times10^{-7}$); both are run at their initial and final weights, giving
four conditions. The wedge and full runs share an initialisation, so their
\textsc{initial} columns are identical up to floating point and act as a
built-in control.

\subsection{Targets and their exact flows}

Let $J_\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times P}$ be the sensitivity Jacobian on a
probe grid ($24\times24$ on $[-3.5,3.5]^2$, points beyond radius $3.5$
dropped, $N=408$), $\Pi$ the group average over $C_{64}$ (a finite
quadrature for the $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ Haar average, exact and idempotent by
construction), and $Xf_\theta(x)=\nabla_xf_\theta(x)\cdot(-x_2,x_1)$ the
infinitesimal rotation generator. Write $\delta=f_\theta-\Pi f_\theta$ and
$g=Xf_\theta$. For each target we solve $J_\theta z\approx\text{target}$ for
its minimum-norm least-squares solution via a truncated SVD of $J_\theta$
(relative cutoff $10^{-3}$, column-normalised, following
\texttt{sensitivity\_tools.tangent\_projection}), giving directions $c$
(target $g$) and $b$ (target $\delta$). Steps are taken along $+c$ and
$-b$, so both walks run on a common positive time axis.

Since any $z$ with $J_\theta z\approx\text{target}$ induces the same
first-order change in $f_\theta$, both walks admit an exact prediction for
the induced flow in function space, independent of the SVD truncation and
norm choice (only the parameter-space path length depends on those):
\begin{align}
  \dot f_t = Xf_t &\;\Longrightarrow\; f_t = f_0\circ R_t
  &&\text{(along $+c$; $t=1$ is a $57.3^\circ$ rotation)} \label{eq:app-c-flow}\\
  \dot f_t = -(I-\Pi)f_t &\;\Longrightarrow\; f_t = \Pi f_0+e^{-t}\delta_0
  &&\text{(along $-b$; defect $\to e^{-1}\lVert\delta_0\rVert$ at $t=1$)} \label{eq:app-b-flow}
\end{align}

\subsection{Two integration modes}

Forward Euler, $t=0,\mathrm{d}t,\dots,1$, $\mathrm{d}t=1/200$ ($200$ steps).
\textsc{Resolved} re-solves $z_t$ from $J_{\theta_t}$ at every step, following
the true integral curve. \textsc{Fixed} solves $z_0$ once at $t=0$ and reuses
it, so $k$ steps of $\mathrm{d}t\,z_0$ sum to one step of $k\,\mathrm{d}t\,z_0$
and the path is the straight line $\theta_0+t z_0$. Both modes share the time
grid, targets, and diagnostics, so the two output directories are comparable
panel for panel. Along the \textsc{resolved} path we additionally score the
direction \emph{frozen at $t=0$} against the current target,
$\lVert J_{\theta_t}z_0-\text{target}(\theta_t)\rVert /
\lVert\text{target}(\theta_t)\rVert$ -- exactly the bet \textsc{fixed} is
making -- and, since $z_0$ is already in hand, compute the \textsc{fixed}
trajectory itself for free as an overlay. Cross-checking this overlay against
an independent run of \textsc{fixed} mode agrees to $\sim10^{-13}$ relative
error on $\lVert(I-\Pi)f_t\rVert$ across all eight (condition, target) pairs.

\subsection{Diagnostics}

At every one of the $201$ stops, for each condition and target, we record:
the non-invariant component $\lVert(I-\Pi)f_t\rVert$ and its relative size,
against the exact prediction; the function-level rotation-invariance error
$\mathcal{E}(T)=\max_\alpha\lvert f_t(R_\alpha x)-f_t(x)\rvert$ over $40$
angles fixed across the walk; distance from $f_t$ to the exact trajectory and
to the fixed endpoint target ($f_0\circ R_1$ or $\Pi f_0$), normalised by
$\lVert f_0\rVert$; the realisation error $\lVert J_{\theta_t}z-g\rVert/
\lVert g\rVert$ for the direction taken and (\textsc{resolved} only) for the
direction frozen at $t=0$; task BCE and accuracy, plus the loss/accuracy of
the exactly projected model $\Pi f_{\theta_t}$; and the distance travelled and
path length in parameter space.

In addition we evaluate $f_t$ on a single orbit $r=1$ ($=r_{\rm inner}$,
where the label rule switches sign) at $360$ angles and record the angular
deviation profile $d_t(\theta)=f_t(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)-\langle
f_t(r,\cdot)\rangle_\theta$ -- exactly $(I-\Pi)f_t$ restricted to that orbit
-- with predictions $d_t(\theta)=d_0(\theta+t)$ (phase shift, along $c$) and
$d_t(\theta)=e^{-t}d_0(\theta)$ (shape-preserving contraction, along $-b$):
the cleanest empirical separation of "moved along the orbit" from
"symmetrised", since the first changes phase with no amplitude change and the
second the reverse.

\subsection{Figures}

Each output directory contains, for both targets and all four conditions:
\texttt{angular\_deviation\_flow} (profile $d_t(\theta)$ at the six snapshot
times, rows $=\{c,-b\}$, dotted $=$ exact prediction);
\texttt{angular\_deviation\_heatmap} (the same profile as a surface over
$(\theta,t)$ using all $201$ stops); \texttt{angular\_deviation\_amplitude}
(RMS$_\theta(d_t)/$RMS$_\theta(d_0)$ and residual from each prediction);
\texttt{equivariance\_flow\_\{c,b\}} (the scalar diagnostics against $t$);
\texttt{flow\_vs\_fixed\_step\_\{c,b\}} (this run's walk, solid, against the
other mode's, dashed); \texttt{decision\_boundary\_flow\_\{c,b\}} (the
decision boundary at the six snapshot times, rows $=$ condition, columns
$=t$).
